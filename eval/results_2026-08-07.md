# Frozen-corpus eval results -- 2026-08-07

Method: `tests/test_eval_frozen_corpus.py`, per the scope doc's eval design.
Corpus: `data/sample_run_2026-08-07.json` (18 real events, live-fetched
2026-08-07). Ground truth defined independently by reading the corpus data,
not the briefing output. Scored: recall (did every event's link get cited),
faithfulness (did any cited URL not belong to a real event in the corpus),
and whether the "no fabricated trends" guardrail held (no unhedged trend
claim, since zero companies had >=2-source support in this corpus).

## What this actually shows

- The `max_tokens` bug (trial 1) was a real, deterministic failure -- fixed,
  verified fixed (no truncation in any of trials 2-5).
- Everything past that is genuine LLM sampling variance, not a bug: the
  same corpus, same prompt, same model produced one run with a fabricated
  URL and incomplete recall, and three runs with excellent recall and no
  fabrication. A single-trial eval would have reported either "broken" or
  "perfect" depending on luck -- neither is the honest answer.
- The trend/no-fabrication guardrail (the doc's specific concern: "explicit
  refusal/flag when a trend isn't supported by >=2 independent sources")
  held in every trial, including the one with citation problems. The
  guardrail logic is enforced upstream in code (Analyst agent's `is_trend`
  flag), not left to the model, which is likely why it's more robust than
  citation completeness, which depends entirely on the model transcribing
  URLs correctly.

## What I'd change at scale (case-study material)

1. **Post-generation citation validation.** Before storing a briefing,
   programmatically extract every URL and check it against the set of URLs
   actually in this run's corpus (exactly what the eval harness does).
   Reject/regenerate on any mismatch instead of trusting the model to copy
   URLs correctly every time -- turns a sampling risk into a hard guarantee.
2. **Lower temperature (or temperature=0) for the Briefing agent.** This is
   citation transcription, not creative writing; there's no real upside to
   sampling diversity here and a plausible downside (this eval's trial 2).
3. **Run the eval on every corpus update, not just once.** A single frozen
   day is a start, not sufficient evidence for a "done" eval per the doc's
   own standard -- would want this wired as a repeatable check (N trials,
   report the range) each time the prompt or model changes.
